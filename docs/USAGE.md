# See what the cleanup costs

Open **Settings → Usage & estimated cost**. The section starts collapsed. Choose **1, 7 or 30 days**, **Hourly** or **Daily**, and **All models** or an individual model. Switch the chart between tokens and estimated USD. Select a time bucket for its totals. Per-model rows show uncached input, cache reads, cache writes and output.

Only BurnZombies-owned background reviews are counted. Ordinary chats are not monitored for billing. Passive observation makes no model calls; an empty or previously reviewed queue starts no review. Hunts are serialized and automatically scheduled every four hours by default; Settings can change the interval or turn the schedule off. Explicit manual hunts can start sooner. A hunt may make multiple focused harness invocations to cover its ready evidence types, but visits each frozen revision once rather than polling a model for progress. There is no fixed daily token or review-count cutoff. Each review receives instructions and tools for its current queue type, without duplicating the full playbook. Up to two storage branches fit the two-inspection limit; successful recommendation/exploration receipts acknowledge only the branch actually handled. Untouched work stays queued. Repeated read-only attempts without progress back off from one to six hours, while fresh evidence can proceed. Each focused invocation still has an eight-tool-call and three-minute limit. The activity log and animated scene make no model calls of their own. Failure pauses future reviews instead of retrying or switching accounts/models.

This controls unnecessary work rather than promising a fixed daily bill: a busy Mac with new evidence can still consume tokens. Input, cached input, output and per-model cost remain visible in Settings. Existing usage receipts survive upgrades; the previous daily limit does not erase or reset accounting. Upgrades recover old discovery branches that were offered but left uninspected without retrying uncertain destructive work.

The local private `review-usage.json` keeps 32 days of receipts, including failed reviews and reported partial usage. Model attribution and price snapshots are saved per model per review; changing the selected model does not reprice earlier reviews. Legacy totals without a model remain readable and unpriced. Unknown models, unsupported pricing tiers and missing counters are not presented as zero-cost usage.

Prices are approximate **standard global API equivalents**, not a conversion to Codex/Claude subscription credits. The rate card is dated **2026-10-27**, from [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) and [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing). Known context thresholds, reported service tiers and cache writes are included. Cache writes use the shorter TTL rate unless a longer TTL is reported; regional premiums, negotiated pricing and hidden approval/model overhead are not inferred. Output includes reasoning when the harness includes it in output counters.

Codex's ephemeral app-server reports its resolved model and cumulative counters. BurnZombies stores positive deltas, not repeated cumulative totals, and pauses on a reroute rather than silently switching models. Claude's final `modelUsage` provides model-separated input, cache and output counts. Other adapters keep unavailable usage unknown until they expose a compatible report.

Storage inspection has a 15-second transport deadline to accommodate the bounded eight-second Mole analyzer. A native recommendation-text validation refusal leaves that branch pending with backoff; it never acknowledges completion. Only exact-attempt typed refusal receipts permit this deferral. Transport, permission, account, protocol and destructive-operation failures still pause reviews.
